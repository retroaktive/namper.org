02 · RIG & SIGNAL CHAIN

Building the sound.

The Rig tab is where your signal chain lives: blocks you add and drag around, a NAM amp profile at the heart of it, a cab impulse response after it, and a master section everything sums into. This page covers all of it — up to four rigs, four parallel chains inside each one, and the presets that recall the lot.

02.1The blocks palette

A chain starts empty and you fill it. Tap the dashed ADD chip at the end of a chain and the palette opens, grouped into sections. Pick a block and it drops into the chain at that point — and its editor opens straight away, so you land on the knobs rather than on an untouched chip.

SectionBlocks
Amp & CabAmplifier, Cabinet
Delay & ReverbDelay, Freeze, Reverb
DriveDrive, Lo-fi, Saturator
DynamicsCompressor, De-Esser, Gate, Limiter, Multiband, Swell, Transient, Vocal
EQ & FilterEqualizer, Wah
ModulationChorus, Doubler, Ensemble, Flanger, Phaser, Rotary, Slicer, Tremolo
Pitch12-string, Autotune, Harmony, Octaver, Pitch, Synth, Vocoder
UtilityFB Guard, FX Loop, Send, Volume
Pluginsthe AU Plugins submenu on iOS, the installed AAP effects on Android

The amp and its cab come first; every other section, and the blocks inside it, is in alphabetical order. A block that has presets opens a submenu instead of dropping straight in: Default settings, the factory and saved presets for that block, and under From rig presets the same block lifted out of a rig preset — the drive from one rig, the delay from another.

Nothing stops you adding the same block twice. Duplicates are numbered as they arrive — GATE 1, GATE 2 — and block names are always drawn in capitals on the chips. Two drives in a row, a gate at the front and another after the amp: all fine.

A block that has no parameters of its own says so plainly when you open it: Nothing to tweak here. That is the block working normally, not a loading state.

There is no duplicate

NAMper has no copy or duplicate action for blocks, anywhere. To get a second block with the same settings, add a fresh one and dial it in. To reuse a whole chain, save it as a preset and build the next rig from that preset instead.

The newer blocks

Twelve blocks joined the palette most recently. What each one is for, in a sentence or two:

BlockWhat it does
DoublerOne take heard as two players: a copy a few cents flat and one as many sharp, each a little late, panned out to the sides with the dry in the middle. Nothing moves, so it is not a chorus. Detune, Delay, Width, Mix.
SwellThe violin swell without a volume pedal: every new note fades in over the Attack time. Sens decides how hard a pick must be to count as a new note, Depth where the fade starts.
Lo-fiFour ways to wreck a signal on its type chips — Ring mod, Crusher, Telephone and Megaphone — each with its own knobs and a Mix.
FreezeA note held for ever: switch Freeze on (a footswitch, a MIDI button) and the last quarter second becomes an endless wash under the dry while you play on top. Attack, Release, Level, Tone.
TransientThe pick and the ring of a note as two knobs, with no threshold: Attack pushes the pick up or softens it, Sustain lifts the ring or shortens it.
SlicerA gate that keeps time: sixteen steps, open or shut by the Pattern. Sync takes the step from the set tempo; Depth is how shut a step is, Edge how fast the gate moves.
MultibandThree bands split at Lo XOver and Hi XOver, each compressed behind one knob — Low, Mid, High — with a gain per band and shared Attack and Release. It evens the boom without pumping the top: a bass, an acoustic, a whole mix.
VocalA voice's channel strip in the order a studio runs it: HPF, De-ess, Comp, Low, Presence, Air, Warmth and Output.
12-stringThe courses doubled: an octave string for what lies below Split and a unison string a few cents sharp over the whole. Octave, Unison, Detune, Tone.
SynthA monophonic synth that follows the string: a Wave through a ladder filter (Cutoff, Reso, Env), moved by Octave and Interval. Note picks the Loudest string or the Lowest, so a synth bass under chords stays on the root.
VocoderSixteen bands put a voice's vowels on another sound. With Voice on This input the block sits on the vocal and plays its own Carrier — the robot; pointed at another rig it sits on the guitar and takes the voice from that rig — the talkbox. Formant, Response, Sibilance, Octave.
FX LoopOutboard gear in the chain: the block sends to one of the interface's output pairs (Out) and takes the return on one of its inputs (In). Send and Return trim the two sides, Stereo return reads two inputs, Phase flips the return. For a parallel loop (a Mix under 100 %) set Latency to the interface's round trip so the dry does not hollow out.

The Amplifier block's Built-in side is NAMper's own tube amp, with the knobs you expect: Gain, Bass, Mid, Treble, Presence and Master, and seven voicings on the Model chips. It needs no download and no account, which is why it carries the show when you are offline; the NAM chip beside it swaps in a capture — see below.

Saturator is where the old Tube Sat and Tape Sat went: one block, Tube, Tape or a NAM capture of an outboard box on its type chips. Ensemble is one instrument heard as several players across the panorama — no LFO, nothing detuned, and the mono sum is the input; Velvet or Allpass on its mode chips.

Drive, dynamics and time

The Drive block's built-in side holds ten circuits in four families, picked from a list, and each family wears its own knobs: a boost Gain, Bass, Treble, Level and Mix (the clean guitar blended back in); an overdrive Gain, Tone, Level, Mix; a distortion Gain, Tone, Bass, Treble, Level; a fuzz Gain, Tone, Bias (the operating point), Voltage (the battery — past the middle it sags and gates) and Level. The ADD menu lists one Drive, and the chain names the block by its circuit, so a stack reads TREBLE BOOST, TUBE, FUZZ GE.

Every Compressor face now also carries Knee, Mix (less than all of it is parallel compression) and HPF, a high-pass on the detector only, so the lows do not pump the rest.

The Delay gained a second tap at a ratio of the time — Tap 2 with its Tap level, for dual and rhythmic delays — plus Reverse, Mod on the repeats, Low cut and High cut inside the feedback loop (each repeat darker or thinner than the last) and Duck, which holds the repeats down while you play and lets them up in the gaps.

The Reverb has two new types. Gated is the eighties drum room: a dense plate cut off by a gate that opens on a hit, holds for Hold and closes over Release. Reverse plays the last Time seconds backwards, continuously, so a decaying note comes back as a swell; Regen feeds it back. On Cloud the shimmer's Interval is now a choice: +8ve, +5th, an octave and a fifth, or -8ve.

The Phaser's Mode adds Vibe — four staggered stages swept by the lamp's waveform, the throb rather than the notches — and Vibrato, the Vibe heard wet only. The Tremolo adds Harmonic: the signal split at 800 Hz and the halves swung against each other, a tremolo that reads as a slow phaser.

Pitch and voice

Harmony sings up to four voices, for a singer or a guitar. Key from says where the key comes from: Set key uses the key list, Backing track follows the song's detected key, This input learns it from what is sung into this chain, and the other rigs are listed by name. Voice 1…Voice 4 are each an interval off the sung note, or OFF, so a third, a fifth and an octave make a chord. The voices keep the singer's formants — a fifth up still sounds like the same person — and Formant moves them on purpose. Choir turns every voice into a section of four singers, each drifting on its own, and Spread pans them across the stereo field.

Autotune takes its key from Chromatic, Backing track or Set key, keeps the singer's formants and lets consonants through. Besides Speed and Amount it now has the singer's own controls: Humanize (a held note retunes slower), Vibrato (how much of the singer's vibrato survives), Flex (a note further off than this is left alone — a scoop on purpose), Formant and Tuning, the reference A.

The Pitch block has three engines: Mono for single-note lines and bends, Poly for chords, and Voice for a singer — the vowels keep their shape an octave up or down, and Formant (on that engine only) makes a bigger or a smaller singer at the same pitch. Control on Pedal turns it into a whammy: the interval slides from Heel to Toe as an expression pedal moves Pedal.

Mix, tempo sync and tails

Every block has a Mix. The ones built around a blend carry it on their own face — a delay's Mix, a harmoniser's Dry, a freeze's Level; every other block, a hosted plugin included, gets a Mix knob as its last knob: the block blended with its own input, so a tremolo can half-chop and an amp can carry a little direct signal. The dry is delayed to match the block, so nothing comb-filters, and scenes, presets and MIDI treat it like any other knob.

Tempo sync is a switch. The delay, the tremolo, the slicer, the phaser, the flanger, the chorus and the rotary each have a Sync switch. On, the rate or time knob turns into note divisions (1/1 to 1/16, dotted and triplet on the delay) that follow the set tempo; off, it is plain Hz or ms again.

Tails ring out. A delay, a reverb, a freeze, an FX loop or a hosted plugin that a scene or a footswitch bypasses keeps ringing over the dry until it is quiet, and a preset switch lets the old rig's tails play on for up to eight seconds instead of cutting them. The switch for this is Tails on a switch in Settings → Audio, on by default.

Harmony that listens to another rig

Harmony's Key from is not a routing control — it is where the harmoniser takes its key from. On This input it learns the key from the signal in its own chain; point it at another rig by name and a vocal line can be harmonised against what the guitar rig is playing.

02.3The seven EQ models

There is one Equalizer block, and inside it seven completely different equalisers. Switching the model swaps the knob set as well as the sound, so pick the one that matches the job — a quick tone tweak, a surgical cut, or a mastering-style shelf.

ModelWhat it isKnobs
Tone Classic three-band — low shelf at 100 Hz, peak at 800 Hz, high shelf at 3 kHz. These are the same filters as the old three-band EQ, so older rigs sound unchanged. Bass, Mid, Treble, each ±12 dB
Para Full parametric — the same three bands, but every frequency moves and the mid gets a Q control. Low, Mid, High, Lo Freq 40–400, Mid Freq 200–5000, Hi Freq 1k–12k, Q 0.3–4
Passive The classic passive program-EQ trick: a low shelf boost plus a slightly higher cut shelf, so you boost and attenuate at once for a tight low end — with a wide high bell and a high shelf cut on top. Every block sheet, this one included, has horizontal IN and OUT meters. On an EQ they are worth watching: a few decibels of boost on three bands adds up fast, and the meters tell you whether the next block is being hit harder than you meant.

02.4NAM captures

The Amplifier block is one block with two sources. Two chips at the top of its sheet choose between them: Built-in is NAMper's own amp simulation with the seven voicings listed above, NAM is the Neural Amp Modeler player running a .nam capture of a real amplifier. Both live in the same block, so a preset carries the voicing it was built on and the capture it was voiced around, and switching between them is one tap.

The capture side

On the NAM side the sheet shows the model's name (or No model loaded), the @author and an arrow that opens the tone on tone3000.com, the cover art on the left, and three ways to get a capture on the right: Browse TONE3000, Library and Load file. Underneath are Input and Output, both −20 to +20 dB and starting at 0. Input pushes the capture harder, which is how you get more or less breakup out of a profile; Output trims the result afterwards.

Levels are measured, not guessed: both sources are unity on the same reference DI, so a preset's output gain means the same thing whichever one is playing, and swapping profiles does not throw your levels around.

Load file takes several .nam files at once. Pick more than one and the app asks Combine the models into a bank? — Combine makes a bank of them, Only the first loads one.

A capture is mono

A NAM capture is a mono device — a stereo signal arriving at the Amplifier block is summed before it goes in. Put your stereo effects after the amp, not before it.

Banks and the Model knob

A TONE3000 tone often ships many models: the same amp through different microphones, channels or cabinets. Download them together and they become the block's bank. The sheet then reads Bank: 6 (or Bank: 4 of 6 when some are left out of the sweep) and a Model knob appears between the trims, reading 1/6 · SM57 as it turns. It is an ordinary knob, so a long press offers MIDI Learn: one CC then walks the whole bank from your pedalboard, and the change is crossfaded rather than cut, so nothing clicks or drops out.

Choosing what goes in the bank

A tone with more than one model opens Choose a model, and in that sheet the ticks are the bank. They start on the models the block already carries, so the sheet opens on what you have rather than on nothing, and the line at the top reads Bank: 3 models — the Model knob steps through them. Tick and untick to shape the sweep: Select all 6 ticks the lot and turns into Deselect all — neither of them downloads anything. The button underneath is the one that acts, and only on what is ticked: Download 5 selected into the bank. While the ticks still match the bank it stays quiet, because there is nothing to fetch.

That is how a bank is trimmed as well as built: open the picker again, untick the models you never reach for, download, and the sweep is rewritten around what is left — files you already have are not fetched twice. A tap on a model's name is the other path, and the sheet says what it costs under the list: One model plays alone: choosing it clears the bank of 3. Models the site lists but you do not have yet are dimmed with a cloud icon until you tick them.

Pedals and outboard: captures in other blocks

There is no separate capture block any more: the blocks that model a kind of gear carry a capture side of their own. The Drive takes captures of pedals, the Saturator and the Compressor take outboard boxes — each with the same NAM chip, trims and bank Model knob as the amp. You do not have to place them yourself: load a pedal capture while an Amplifier is aimed at, and NAMper puts it in the rig's Drive — inserting one right before the amp if there is none — and opens it for you; an outboard box lands in a Saturator the same way. Real outboard gear, rather than a capture of it, goes into the chain through the FX Loop block. Older presets that used a NAM block open with it converted.

The library

The Library button opens a sheet titled NAM Profiles — everything you have downloaded, available offline. It has five sides, one per kind of block: Amp and Drive for captures, Outboard for the saturator and the compressor, Cab for cabinet impulses and Reverb for the reverb's — spaces, outboard boxes and experiments. A block only takes one of the five, so they are never listed together: the block you opened the library from picks the side and keeps it, and with no block to aim at, chips across the top name the five with a count each. The impulses shipped with the app sit on their sides under Built-in, next to the ones you download.

Inside a side, tones are grouped by what was captured: Built-in first, then Amplifiers, Pedals, Outboard, Experimental, Cabinets, Spaces and Other. Each row is a tone, not a file: cover art, title, @author and how many models it ships. A tap loads it — a tone with one model plays alone, a tone with several becomes the block's bank — and the tone lands where it belongs: an amp in the Amplifier block, a pedal in the Drive before it, an outboard box in the Saturator, a cabinet in the Cabinet after it, a space in the Reverb.

The chevron opens the tone's models. In the tone that is the block's bank each model carries a checkmark: it keeps that model in the Model knob's sweep, and a tap moves the knob onto it. In any other tone a tap on a model plays that model alone and clears the bank. Models the site lists but you do not have yet are dimmed with a cloud icon; a tap fetches one, and in the bank tone it joins the sweep.

On the right of every tone row: the star (a favourite — starring a tone stars all of its models) and the refresh button (Refresh from TONE3000, which re-fetches the metadata, the cover and the files). A long press, or a right-click on a row, offers Open on TONE3000, Share .nam file / Share IR file and Delete. The toolbar has Import files… — a multi-select picker that takes .nam captures and .wav impulses — along with Export all (N files) and Update all.

Empty, the NAM side reads Profiles you download from TONE3000 collect here.; the impulse sides always hold at least the built-ins. If a model cannot be read you get Could not load amp model {filename}. — usually a file that was moved or deleted out from under the rig.

Capture your own gear

Capture a NAM profile… in the library's toolbar makes a capture of your own amp, pedal or whole rig on the device, with nothing but an audio interface. It is the reamp loop every NAM capture is made from: the signal leaves the interface on one output, goes through the gear and comes back on one input. The Capture window picks that output and that input by the interface's own channel names; while it is open the live rig is cut, so a looped-back rig cannot feed itself and howl. Set the send level, and Check levels plays the head of the signal with two meters and a note on how much to move the send — the return should peak near −6 dBFS and never clip. If the input already carries sound the send did not cause — the gear feeding back through the loop, or a host playing its track through the plug-in — the note says so: turn the gear's input or the send down, or stop playback, and check again.

Start capture (190 s) plays the NAM trainer's own standardized signal, v3_0_0.wav, so a take made here trains here and in any other NAM trainer as well. The take is looked at before anything is trained: the blips near the start locate the round trip, two copies of the same stretch at both ends measure the drift between two clocks, and a take where nothing came back, the return clipped or the loop changed nothing is refused. Train runs on the device — Nano, Feather (the default), Lite or Standard, for as many epochs as you set — and shows the epoch and the ESR on audio the trainer never saw (0.02 is a good capture). The result lands in the library as your own tone, tagged with its gear, and Share .nam file hands it to any NAM player.

Would rather train elsewhere? Share the take pair (on the Mac, Save the take pair…) hands over the signal and the return as 48 kHz WAV files — exactly what TONE3000's trainer and the NAM trainer take.

02.5TONE3000

TONE3000 is a free library of captures, and NAMper pulls from it directly. There is no in-app browse screen: Browse TONE3000 opens tone3000.com inside a secure web sheet. You search and filter on the website itself, pick a tone, and the app takes it from there — while it fetches, the button reads Loading… and is disabled.

Signing in is an ordinary web sign-in, not a pasted API key, and if you are already logged in to tone3000.com in the browser the app reuses that session. NAMper never asks for it at launch — the factory presets play on the built-in amps and the bundled cabinets, and the sign-in happens the first time you press Browse TONE3000 in a block that takes a capture. Settings has no TONE3000 page.

What the catalogue offers depends on the block that opened it. From an Amplifier it is filtered server-side to A2-architecture .nam captures of amps and full rigs, from a Drive to pedals, and from a Saturator or a Compressor to outboard gear. From a Cabinet block it offers the site's cabinet impulses, and from an Reverb its spaces — .wav files, not captures. That filter is fixed, not a setting you can change.

When the tone you picked has one model, it lands straight in the block with its cover art and metadata. When it has several, a picker opens: tap one to play it alone, or tick a few and Download {n} selected into the bank — the whole tone is Download all {n} models into the bank. Everything lands in the app's tones/ folder and joins the library.

The factory presets, on captures or built-in

The Settings index carries one switch, under FACTORY PRESETS: Built-in amps or TONE3000 captures. The four factory rigs were voiced around four public captures — a Fender Deluxe Reverb, a Marshall Plexi, a Victory Kraken and a VOX AC30 — and the switch decides which of the two the bank plays. NAMper offers the choice once, the first time you pick a capture of your own.

Playing offline

Anything already downloaded keeps working with no network at all. An Amplifier block whose capture is not cached plays its own built-in voicing meanwhile and says so (Not downloaded yet — the built-in amp plays meanwhile), with the Cabinet after it awake for the built-in amp. Sign in later and those blocks resolve to the real captures in place — you do not have to rebuild anything.

There is no sign-out

NAMper has no TONE3000 sign-out button. Once the session exists, the only way to clear it is to delete the app. Worth knowing before you sign in on a borrowed device.

When something goes wrong the app says which of the four things it is: TONE3000: sign in to load %@., TONE3000: the server had a problem (HTTP %lld) — try again later., TONE3000: unexpected reply for %@. and TONE3000: no connection — check the network.

Downloads and backups

The tones/ folder is excluded from iCloud backup on purpose — a restore-from-backup will not bring your downloaded profiles with it, because TONE3000 can fetch them again. If a particular capture matters to you, export it from the NAM Profiles sheet and keep the file.

02.6Cabs and IRs

The Cabinet block plays an impulse response — a speaker cabinet captured with a microphone. Its sheet is built like a capture block's: the impulse's name across the top with the @author, an arrow to the tone's page and Clear IR beside it, the cover art on the left, and three ways to load one on the right — Browse TONE3000, Library and Load file. Under them sits the block's own knob, Level, −12 to +12 dB.

A Reverb on its IR type loads a space rather than a cabinet, from the library's Spaces side: the bundled ones, what you download, your own files. Loading a space into any Reverb turns it to the IR type.

Browse TONE3000 opens the site's cabinets from a Cabinet and its spaces from an Reverb. A tone with several impulses opens the same picker the captures use: play one, or tick a few and download them together. What happens next is where the two blocks part company. In an Reverb the ticked impulses become a bank like an amp's — Bank: 6 and a Model knob beside Mix, one space at a time, MIDI Learn and all. A Cabinet does not step through them: every impulse it takes becomes a slot, and the slots play together.

Several impulses in one cab

Two microphones on one cabinet, a 4×12 and a 1×12 side by side, a whole cabinet pack — one Cabinet block holds them all. Add beside the chips offers the three usual doors — From the Library, Browse TONE3000 and Load file — and whatever comes through one joins as a slot with a colour of its own. A tone with several impulses fills the block in one go: ticked in the picker, they arrive as slots. The response curves above the chips gain a trace per slot and a white one over them — the composite, which is what you hear.

Every chip carries that colour, the impulse's name and a badge with the level the slot plays at. A tap on the chip opens its card; a tap on the badge takes the slot out of the mix or brings it back; a long press solos it — the soloed slot is named on a line of its own under the chips, with a cross that lets the rest back in. A pack with more than a dozen impulses scrolls in a box of its own rather than pushing the sheet down.

The field underneath is the mix itself. The slots sit around a circle, the puck between them, and dragging the puck moves the balance the way moving a microphone would — no menus, no numbers. Blend X and Blend Y are ordinary parameters, so a long press on the puck offers MIDI Learn for each: two pedals, and the microphone position becomes something you play. The mix is re-rendered as you drag and handed to the convolver as a crossfade rather than a swap, so moving the puck mid-song does not click.

The card belongs to the selected slot: Trim, Pan and Delay under a row of buttons. NAMper measures how far apart the impulses sit and aligns them for you, and Auto holds that measurement until you turn Delay yourself — tap it again to hand the alignment back. The buttons beside it mute the slot, invert its polarity (worth a try whenever two microphones on one speaker sound thin) and remove it.

Load file opens a file picker for .wav and other audio; whatever you choose is copied into the app's irs/ folder, so it stays available even if you delete the original. A file loaded into a Cabinet lands on the library's Cab side, one loaded into an Reverb on its Reverb side.

Not every amp needs a cab

Plenty of TONE3000 captures are taken from the speaker output, cab and microphone included; putting another cab after one of those makes the sound dull and boxy. NAMper reads that from the tone and handles it: a full-rig capture rests the Cabinet right after the amp, a bare head or a DI wakes it. A Cab left resting with no impulse in it is not even drawn in the chain — load an impulse into it, or switch the amp back to a bare head, and it comes straight back. The Cab's own footswitch always has the last word.

An empty Cabinet block says why it might stay empty: No IR loaded — amp+cab NAM profiles don't need one. An empty Reverb reads No IR loaded — pick a space or a file instead.

IR problems show as IR file missing: {name}, Could not read IR {name}. or Could not import {name}.

02.7Presets

A rig preset stores the whole chain — blocks, models, knob positions, scenes, the amp profile, the cab. Presets live on MIDI program-change slots in four banks of 128: Factory and User 1–3. Save one from the + on the PRESETS card, or from the preset manager: the Save preset sheet asks for everything a preset has.

FieldWhat it sets
NameLeft empty, it saves as Preset.
BankWhich user bank it goes into.
ProgramThe program-change number, starting on the first free one. A menu lists all 128; a taken number says which preset it would replace, a free one says free.
CategoriesChips to tick — the same categories the preset lists filter by.
TagsYour own words, comma-separated; search finds them.

Banks

The bank chip beside the PRESETS title, and in the preset manager, says which bank is up and switches it. The bank that is up is what the lists show and what program change n plays. From a pedalboard, MIDI Bank Select (CC 0) picks it — 0 is Factory, 1–3 the user banks — and Next preset bank / Previous preset bank can be learned onto any switch. A preset's menu has Move to bank for your own and Copy to bank for a factory one. A setlist is not tied to a bank: its presets play whichever bank is up.

The factory bank is read-only

Update with current rig on a factory preset asks first, then Save a copy puts your version into the user bank on its first free number, switches to that bank and plays it; renaming a factory preset makes the same copy. Update the same factory preset again and it edits that copy — and every song that recalled the factory preset recalls your copy from then on, scenes included.

Search and categories

The rail and the manager wear the same search field and category chips. Search matches names and tags, so "vocal" pulls up the whole vocal bank and "delay" every echo-flavoured rig. The chips narrow the list: All, Mine (only your own presets), then the categories — Clean, Lead, Blues, Ambient, Metal, Bass, Vocal, Utility and more.

The rail has two faces

The PRESETS card — the rail beside the chain on a tablet and on a phone held upright, however many rigs there are; the strip under the chain on a phone on its side — shows one of two things, and which one depends on whether a setlist is LIVE:

  • No setlist live — the bank: every preset on its MIDI PC slot, with its cover, the search and the category chips, and a row menu holding Update with current rig, Rename…, Assign to set…, Move to bank, Share and Delete. Program change n applies preset n of the bank that is up.
  • A setlist live — the set: the set's name, a song stepper (‹ name ›, and the name itself drops down the whole list to jump), and one pad per rig preset the current song brings up — not the 128 slots, but the four or eight sounds this number actually needs — with the bank under them. Program change n now presses stage button n: the songs while none is picked, then the picked song's own pads.

On stage the second face is the one that matters: the song is picked once, and what you reach for between choruses is this song's own sounds. Each pad carries the preset's name and its scene row — only the scenes that preset holds — with the entry's own scene outlined and the whole pad lit while it is the one playing. A tap on the pad brings that entry up exactly as its song lists it; a tap on a letter makes that scene the entry's own, and tapping the chosen letter again clears it.

The set is edited right there. Drag a preset from the bank below onto a pad (hold, then move — a plain swipe still scrolls) and it takes that pad's place, keeping its scene; drop it on an empty slot and it joins the song. A preset's menu has Add to this song, which lists the song's pads and a New pad. A pad's own long-press menu has Replace sound… (a picker across every bank), Current rig (the rig as it stands saved into the pad's preset), Clear pad and MIDI Learn. A long press on an empty slot offers Choose preset… and Current rig.

The stack icon in the set panel's header opens the full preset library anyway, and ⊗ leaves the setlist: the rail goes back to the bank, and so do the program numbers.

Between the chain card and the presets sit a grip and two chevrons. Drag the grip to give the chain or the presets more room, double-tap it to go back to the page's own split; a chevron folds the chain or the presets into a thin strip, and a tap on the strip brings it back.

Updating and managing

Overwriting is always confirmed. Update with current rig on one of your own presets raises Overwrite “{name}” with the current rig? with Overwrite and Cancel. Rename… also sets the program number: the preset already holding that number takes the old one in exchange. The manager itself has the nav title Presets, the bank chip, the search and the category chips, and two sections: Rigs · MIDI PC slots, and Everything at once → Global snapshots, described as rig, looper, sampler, setlist and control map saved and restored together.

Delete is not confirmed

Delete in the preset row menu takes effect immediately, with no "are you sure". Export a backup before a clear-out.

Import and export

The manager's import/export menu has Import preset / bank / backup…, Export rig presets (which writes NAMper Presets.namper) and Export EVERYTHING (full backup) (which writes NAMper Backup.namper). Note that a full backup carries parameters and references but not sampler kit audio.

The file extension is .namper. Tap one in Files, or receive one over AirDrop, and NAMper imports it and opens the preset manager. Every imported preset gets a fresh slot, so an import can never silently overwrite something you already had.

The factory bank

The Factory bank ships all 128 slots filled: the classic four on PC 0–3 — Clean, Crunch, Lead and Ambient, each voiced around a real capture — followed by themed rigs that walk through styles and eras, acoustic instruments, a bass bank, a vocal bank and utility chains. The newer blocks play their part in it: swells and freezes in the ambient rigs, a doubled vocal, a twelve-string, a synth bass and vocal strips. Every factory rig plays on the built-in amps and the bundled cabinets from the first second, and each is marked FACTORY in the manager. Hide factory presets in the manager's import/export menu takes the bank out of the bank menu; the same switch brings it back. Settings → RESET → Reset to factory presets clears your own presets; the dialog warns Delete ALL user presets? The four factory rigs come back.

Scenes and the morph pedal

Each rig has four scenes, A–D: bypass and knob positions for every block, with the chain and the loaded models left alone, so switching one is gapless. The scene that is lit follows your edits — a knob turned while A is up belongs to A from then on, so a tweak is never lost by switching away.

An expression pedal can also morph between two scenes. In Settings → Control, give a CC row the action Scenes → Morph with a pedal and pick the pair: every knob the two scenes disagree on follows the pedal between its two values, and a switch, a mode or a bypass flips at the halfway point — a clean-to-lead ride where the drive, the delay mix and the reverb all come up under one foot.

The other tabs have preset sheets of their own, reached from their PRESETS chip and all sharing the same design: Looper Presets, Sampler Kits, Perform Presets, Control Presets and Global Snapshots. Two of them cannot be shared, because they point at files on this device — sampler kits carry their audio, and global snapshots reference models, samples and backing tracks.

02.8Multiple rigs

NAMper runs up to four rigs at once — a guitar and a vocal, a guitar and a bass, or four players through one interface. Add one from the RIG + menu in the SIGNAL CHAIN header.

The menu's Templates section starts you somewhere sensible: Guitar rig, Bass rig, Vocal rig, Acoustic rig and Empty rig. Below that, From preset lists your saved presets by name — or opens a searchable NEW RIG FROM PRESET sheet once there are more than eight of them.

A template is not a bare chain. Each one lands with its four scenes filled and one branch beside the chain: the guitar's wet delay and reverb line (Clean · Crunch · Lead · Ambient), the bass's dirt taken before the amp and merged after it so the low end stays clean (Fingers · Pick · Synth · Dub), the vocal's detuned double (Dry · Verb · Double · Wide), and the acoustic's room, with a body cut and a presence lift instead of an amp (Strum · Fingers · Room · DI). The branch's power switch is part of the scene, so a scene change switches it in and out with everything else — and every template runs on the built-in tube amp and the bundled cabinets, so it plays offline the second it lands.

Rigs are named GUITAR, BASS, VOCAL, ACOUSTIC or RIG n depending on the template; a rig built from a preset takes the preset's name. Headers only appear once you have more than one rig.

Focus, pinning and removal

Tap a rig's header to give it MIDI focus — a badge marks which rig your controller is currently driving. The pin toggle (Pin rig / Unpin rig, captioned "presets keep this rig") makes a rig survive preset switches, which is how you keep a vocal chain intact while you change guitar sounds from a footswitch. A trash button (Remove rig) appears once there is more than one rig.

Layout follows the device: in landscape the rigs stack as rows with each chain running left to right; in portrait they sit side by side with the chains running top to bottom.

Each rig's input and output

The IN connector at the head of a chain is a menu. Its Sound-card input section lists your channels by name — In 1, In 2 and so on — and adds Sampler pads and Loop 1–Loop 4, so a rig can process a loop or the sampler rather than a live input.

The OUT connector opens a sheet headed RIG OUTPUT, with Level and Pan knobs, a Hardware output pair menu (Out 1-2, Out 3-4… — this needs Use all interface outputs switched on in Settings), and Record this rig into → Loop track 1–Loop track 4. The summary row underneath reads Looper: not assigned or, once you have picked some, Looper: track 1, 3.

02.9Parallel branches

A rig is not limited to one line. Each rig can hold up to four chains — the main one plus three branches — so you can split a signal, treat the halves differently and mix them back together. Create one with the branch-icon button under the chain (Add parallel chain).

Its menu has a single section, Branch from, listing each input channel, Sampler pads, Loop 1–Loop 4, and After {Block} for every block on the main chain. That last group is the interesting one: branching after the amp gives you two different reverbs on the same amp tone, branching before it gives you two different amps on the same guitar.

The two anchors

A branch draws as a parallel column with an entry anchor where it leaves the main chain and a merge anchor where it rejoins. The merge anchor shows that branch's level as a percentage, so you can read the balance without opening anything.

GestureWhat it does
Tap an anchorOpens the mixer sheet headed LANE {n} MIX, with Level and Pan
Long-press the entry anchorThe Branch input menu: own input, Sampler pads, Loop 1–Loop 4, Branch after {Block}, and Remove branch
Long-press the merge anchorThe Branch output menu: Into the main mix, Merge before {Block}, Own output 1-2, and Remove branch
Drag an anchorRe-attaches it at a different point along the main chain

Dragging is the fast way to audition: hold the merge anchor, slide it earlier or later in the chain, and hear the branch land before or after the delay without opening a menu.

Switching a branch out

A power switch sits on the branch's own wire, a little way past the fork. Tap it and the whole branch leaves the graph — it is drawn dashed and dim, and nothing in it is processed. The same switch is in the branch's menu and, as Bypass branch / Enable branch, in its mixer sheet and on its strip in the Mix tab.

Scenes remember it, so a parallel amp or a sub-octave can come and go with a footswitch instead of being rebuilt. That is how the factory rig Friday Night Lead works: its octave branch is switched out everywhere except the breakdown scene.

Mixing several branches at once

Put a Volume block at the point where branches merge and it grows an extra section, INCOMING LINES, with one knob per merging branch — Line 1, Line 2 and so on. That gives you a small mixer in the middle of the rig instead of chasing individual anchor sheets.

A branch can also skip the main mix entirely: point its output at Own output 1-2 and it goes straight to its own hardware pair, which needs Use all interface outputs enabled in Settings first.

02.10The master section

The MASTER strip is where everything meets: every rig, the looper and both send returns sum there before the hardware output. Whatever you do to the master, you do to all of it. It used to sit at the foot of the Rig tab; it lives on the Mix tab now, in the column beside the faders — this section is the short version, the desk around it is on that page.

The dashed + bar adds master processing — up to six blocks, from the same families a chain offers, without the amp and the Send. They behave like chain blocks: tap a chip to edit it, long-press for Enable / Bypass and Remove.

Plugins on the master

The master and the two send returns take hosted plugins too: the same Plugins section sits at the foot of their add menus, so a third-party limiter can sit across everything.

There is no always-on master processor here. Saturation is a block you add — Saturator, with Tube, Tape or a NAM capture on its type chips and Drive, Tone, Mix and Level — and limiting is the Limiter block, with Gain, Ceiling (−12 to 0 dB, default −1) and Release. If you have not added them, they are not in the signal path.

Reading the master meters

Stereo peak bars L and R carry ticks at −12 and −6 dB. Beside them sits a BS.1770 loudness reading: a large momentary LUFS number with a smaller line below reading LUFS or LUFS · S <n> for the short-term value. Below −70 LUFS it shows -inf. The fader runs −60 to +12 dB.

Use the peak bars to stay out of clipping and the LUFS number to match your overall level to something else — a backing track, a previous take, another player on the same desk.

The click stays clean

The metronome click is mixed in after the master FX chain, so master reverbs, saturation and limiting never touch it. Set the click level in Settings → METRONOME, and give it its own output pair there if you want it in the in-ears and nowhere else.
