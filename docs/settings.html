09 · SETTINGS REFERENCE

Every switch, in order.

NAMper's Settings sheet is short on purpose. This page walks it card by card, row by row, with the defaults and what each control really does — and ends with the handful of settings that live somewhere else entirely.

09.1Opening Settings

Tap the gear icon in the header of the Rig tab. That is the only way in, and the header only exists on that tab — from Loop, Sample, Perform or Mix you go back to Rig first.

Settings is an index rather than one long sheet: a row for About and seven pages, one tap each, so the thing you came for is on a page about that thing. One choice stays on the index itself, under FACTORY PRESETS: Built-in amps, TONE3000 captures or HyperReal (the chip with the HyperReal mark, and the default) — which amps the factory bank plays; see HyperReal in the factory bank. When the processor cannot carry HyperReal, the bank steps back to the built-in amps and this choice shows it. There is no TONE3000 page — signing in happens in the block that wants a capture.

09.2Audio interface

The AUDIO INTERFACE card is the longest one, and the only one whose settings need a relaunch to take effect. The first four rows are read-only: they tell you what NAMper actually got from the system, which is the fastest way to find out why an interface is not behaving. On a Mac the same page also chooses the interface — the input and output devices, the output pair — see Mac. If the interface drops out, the engine pauses and starts again by itself when it comes back, at the same settings and with the loops kept; meanwhile an iPhone or iPad stays silent, a Mac plays through the system's device and an Android phone through its own.

On Android the page is grouped in five cards: AUDIO INTERFACE (what is plugged in and what the sound is Playing through), CONNECTION (the direct USB driver, the rate, the channels, the buffer), INPUT, LATENCY and PROCESSOR. The rows below are the same.

Use all interface outputsSwitchOff Opens every output pair, so a branch, a rig or the click can own its own pair. Stereo devices do not need it.
Lite NAM modelsSwitchOff TONE3000's A2 captures carry a lighter sub-model; this runs every capture on it — a fraction of the DSP load, a shade less detail. Captures without one are unaffected. Takes effect at once.
Amp modeler on its own threadSwitchOn A HyperReal amp runs on a processor core of its own, two buffers behind the audio: room for several amps at once, for those two buffers of extra latency. If the thread cannot keep up, the amp goes back to the audio thread by itself. Switching it reloads the amps that play. iPhone, iPad and Android; the Mac and the plug-ins have no such switch.
Amp modeler qualityChips: Live, StudioLive How finely a HyperReal amp is computed: Live is lighter on the processor, Studio finer and heavier. A change recompiles the amps that play, with no restart. If a Studio amp keeps the busiest thread past its deadline for two seconds, the amps run in Live and the row reads Studio (running Live: this device cannot keep up) until you pick a quality again. The plug-ins have no such row.
Tails on a switchSwitchOn The blocks a preset switch leaves behind ring out — a delay's repeats, a reverb's decay — for up to eight seconds instead of stopping at the switch, and a delay, reverb, FX loop or plugin that a scene bypasses rings out over the dry (a freeze is held sound, not a tail: it stops). A little more DSP for those seconds.
Round tripReadout + Measure latency…, Reset— Android: the latency from output to input the looper lines overdubs up by, measured with a loop cable or estimated from the streams; kept per interface. How to measure.
Align the rigsSwitchOff Rigs on different inputs are delayed to the slowest of them where the desk sums them, so a guitar and a mic land together. Off, each rig keeps its own latency; the paths of one input always line up either way.
Audio changes need a relaunch

The chips and switches here take effect on the next engine start — some, like the sample rate, restart the engine themselves. If something looks stuck, power the engine off and on before assuming it is broken.

09.3Channel names

CHANNEL NAMES is a text field per input, each row prefixed In 1, In 2 and so on, with the placeholder Guitar, Vocal…. The footnote says what they are for: Input pickers show these names everywhere.

Name them once and those names appear in every input picker in the app — rig inputs, looper track sources, compressor sidechains, the input trim sliders. Channels you leave blank fall back to In <n>.

Worth two minutes

On a multichannel interface, Guitar and Vocal are much easier to pick correctly mid-soundcheck than In 3 and In 4.

09.4Appearance

Chips: System, Light, Dark. The default is System, and appearance changes apply the instant you tap them.

Under the chips are the themes, one choice per polarity: pick a dark theme and a light one, and System wears the dark one when the phone is dark and the light one when it is not. Four dark themes ship — Studio (the app's own), Midnight Violet, Ember and Deep Sea — and three light ones: Concrete, the default, a light grey a shade down from paper; Desk, the paper-white the app first shipped with; and Linen.

Customize colors… copies whatever theme is worn into your own and lets you set each role by hand — the background, the cards, the text, the knobs, the power button and every block family's accent. Corner radius in the same editor scales every rounded corner in the app at once, from sharp (30 %) to very round (200 %); the themes ship at 50 %. A reset row puts your copy back to the theme it came from.

09.5Set

The SET card is read-only. It shows Tempo (as BPM, or — when nothing is set) and Key (such as Am, or —). It is a status readout, not a control.

The footnote points you at the real controls: Tempo: long-press the metronome. Key: the KEY button in the header.

ValueSet it from
TempoLong-press the metronome chip in the Rig header, or the tempo block in the Loop tab. The looper also detects it from your first loop, and selecting a backing track applies the song's tempo. The time signature is on the same tempo sheet — see tempo and the click.
KeyThe KEY chip in the Rig header. Selecting a backing track applies the song's key.

09.6Lyrics

One slider: teleprompter text size, from 16 to 34 pt, default 22 pt, with a live preview line reading This is how your lyrics will look.

Under it, Open the lyrics with the song: on, the teleprompter takes the screen when a song with lyrics starts; off, the tab you were on stays where it is and the Lyrics button opens the words when you want them. On by default.

Changing it re-aims the teleprompter live, so if the lyrics screen is open on another device or you come straight back to it, nothing has to be re-timed. Set it for the distance the phone actually sits from your face on a mic stand, not from the sofa. See lyrics and the teleprompter.

09.7Metronome

RowControlDefaultWhat it does
Click levelSlider, −40…+6 dB≈ −3 dB How loud the click is.
Click outputMenu: Main, then each output pair by the names the interface's driver gives its channels (3 / 4 when it gives none)Main Which hardware output pair carries the click. Shown only when there is more than one pair; picking a non-main pair force-enables Use all interface outputs.
Click channelChips: L+R, L, R (on another pair, its channel numbers)L+R Both channels of the pair, or one of them: a mono click into a single output, the other left for the mix.

The card's footnote is the useful part: The click is mixed in AFTER the master FX chain — reverbs and limiters never touch it. Pick a separate output pair for in-ear monitoring, and a single channel when the click shares the pack with the mix.

That means your master reverb will not smear the click and your master limiter will not duck the band every time it ticks. Send it to its own pair and the drummer hears the click while front of house does not.

The click itself is switched on and off with the metronome chip in the Rig header, or the CLICK chip in the Loop tab — not from here.

09.8Looper

The LOOPER card on the Stage page tunes the looper's AUTO mode.

RowControlDefaultWhat it does
Silence that closes an auto-loopSlider, 0.5–4 beats1 beat How long the quiet after a phrase must last, in beats of the tempo heard in your playing, before the take closes itself. Longer for phrases with gaps, shorter for tight ones.
Signal that starts an auto-loopSlider, −54…−24 dB−40 dB The level your playing has to stay over for a couple of milliseconds before an armed cell starts recording. Lower is more sensitive; a brush of the strings or the room stays under it.
Keep time through the silenceSwitchOn The loop starts playing where it would be had it never stopped, so the count never breaks. Off, it starts from the top when it closes.

09.9Packs

A pack is extra content NAMper downloads only when you ask for it — the Stem splitter that splits a song into its parts is the first. Every pack is free, with no account: Packs are extra content, free to download. They stay on this device and the NAMper plug-ins use them too. They come from packs.namper.org, and the list is read each time the page opens.

Each pack shows its name, its size and a line on what it does, then where it stands:

StateWhat you can do
Not downloadedDownload. A bar shows the progress, with Cancel beside it; every file is checked when it lands.
DownloadedDelete — It can be downloaded again. When a newer version is out, Update appears beside it.
This pack needs a newer NAMper.The pack, or its newer version, was made for a later app. It waits until you update NAMper; a version you already have keeps working.
Download failed: …Try again. A download that stopped halfway carries on from where it was.

On a phone or tablet a pack downloads over Wi-Fi unless Download over mobile data is on; an iPhone or iPad reads Waiting for Wi-Fi meanwhile. A Mac downloads over any network.

The plug-ins do not download packs. Their own Packs page lists what the app has downloaded and uses it: Packs are downloaded in the NAMper app. The plug-in uses the ones that are there.

RowControlDefaultWhat it does

The quick tour is a spotlight over the live Rig tab, counted n/7, and it is hands-on rather than a slideshow: it walks you through the amp card, the signal chain (you really tap a block), the block editor, presets, the other tabs and a closing step. Next, Skip step and End tour are always available, with Finish at the end. It runs on the Rig tab only.

09.11Reset

Two buttons, each behind a confirmation.

ButtonDialogConfirm
Reset to factory presets Delete ALL user presets? The four factory rigs come back. Delete all user presets
Reset all settings Reset every setting (audio, looper, MIDI map, appearance) to defaults? Presets and files stay. Restart the app afterwards. Reset settings

The first one wipes your rig presets and restores the four factory rigs. The second leaves every preset and every file alone and only puts the settings back to their defaults.

Restart after a settings reset

The footnote reads Settings reset takes effect after an app restart., and it means it. Only the appearance change applies immediately — the audio settings need a relaunch before they are truly back at their defaults. Quit NAMper from the app switcher and open it again.

09.12Diagnostics

NAMper keeps a plain-text engine log recording interface routing and peaks. When one exists, this card offers Share engine log and Clear engine log; when it does not, it reads No engine log yet (it records interface routing and peaks).

Share engine log hands the file to the system share sheet — mail it, AirDrop it, or attach it to a support issue. It is the single most useful thing you can include in a bug report about an interface that will not open, a rate that gets refused or an engine that stops on its own, because it records what actually happened at the audio layer.

Clear engine log empties it. Do that immediately before reproducing a problem and the log you send contains that problem and nothing else.

09.13Not in Settings

Three things people look for here and do not find.

Trigger quantize

The QUANT knob and its grid chip live in the Sample tab header, not in Settings — even though both are global rather than per kit or per pad. See trigger quantize.

Tempo and key

Both are header chips on the Rig tab. Settings only reports them, in the SET card.

MIDI

MIDI in has no global settings: no channel selector, no clock sync to set up. It is omni and always on — every channel is accepted (a mapping row can be told to listen to one), every source connects automatically, and the app publishes its own virtual destination named NAMper.

MIDI out has a card, on the MIDI clock page at the foot of the Control page: MIDI OUT, off until you switch it on. It sends a preset's program change, a scene's, and the looper's MIDI cells as notes, to the clock's Destination — Notes channel and Preset channel; Scene as a Program change or a Control change, on the Scene channel (and the Scene CC); Echo to controllers, which sends a move made in the app back to its mapped CC so motor faders and lit switches follow; the Synth channel for the Synth block's notes and its Bend range; and All notes off for a note left sounding.

Nothing to configure

If a pedal is not doing anything, the problem is the mapping rather than a setting. Everything MIDI lives on the Control page of Settings — see MIDI & control.
